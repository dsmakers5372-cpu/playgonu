How Jul-gonu works Warm-up round
  • Each player starts with 4 pieces lined up on their own edge of the board.
  • Move one piece per turn, one step along a line.
  • Trap an enemy piece between two of yours in a straight line to capture it.

Jul-gonu — a Korean Traditional Board Game, Play It Online

Jul-gonu (줄고누) is a traditional Korean board game for two. Each player moves four pieces one step at a time on a 4×4 line board and captures by sandwiching an enemy piece between two of their own. It is also called four-line Gonu, and it is the simplest of the Gonu games to learn. On PlayGonu you can play it against an AI or a friend, with nothing to install.

Gonu and Jul-gonu: where it comes from

Gonu is a family of Korean folk board games. Players draw a board on the ground or on paper and use pebbles or twigs as pieces, so no special equipment is needed. That is why the games spread across the whole country and carried regional names such as goni and kkoni (Gyeonggi), kkon and kkonu (Jeolla), kkon (Gyeongsang) and kkonja (Jeju). The name is thought to come from an old word meaning "to contend". A board carved into an artifact from a Goryeo-era celadon kiln suggests people were playing at least as far back as the Goryeo period (918–1392), and Kim Hong-do's late-Joseon genre painting "Gonu Play" shows a group playing on a board drawn in the dirt.

About twenty kinds of Gonu are known across Korea, named after their board shapes, such as Umul-gonu (well), Hobak-gonu (pumpkin) and Cham-gonu. Jul-gonu is the one played on a grid of crossing lines, and it is named for the number of lines: four lines is Jul-gonu, five is Daseotjul-gonu. The number of pieces and the details differ from region to region. PlayGonu follows the four-line sandwich-capture version that is also described in English-language Wikipedia.

How to play Jul-gonu
  1. Setup: Each player puts 4 pieces on the intersections of their own front row. The first player moves first.
  2. Moving: On your turn, slide one piece one step along a line, horizontally or vertically. No diagonal moves and no jumping.
  3. Capturing: If your move leaves an enemy piece in a straight line between two of your pieces, you remove it. One move can capture in two directions at once.
  4. The safe square: A piece that moves between two enemy pieces on its own is not captured. Only the player who moved can capture.
  5. Winning: Leave your opponent with just 1 piece, or with no piece that can move.
  6. Move limit: If 30 moves in total pass with no capture, the side with more pieces wins; equal pieces is a draw (a PlayGonu rule).

Jul-gonu tips

  • The four corners are safe. A corner piece has no squares on both sides to be sandwiched between, so it can never be captured. Use corners as a base.
  • Keep your pieces together. A lone piece is easy to sandwich. Two pieces side by side protect each other.
  • The square between enemy pieces is a safe zone. Because you cannot be captured when you step in yourself, you can plant a piece deep in enemy territory as a launch point.
  • Threaten two things at once. If one move threatens two captures, your opponent can stop only one.
  • Do not rush when ahead. Being one piece up is enough to win on the 30-move limit.

Frequently asked questions

Are Jul-gonu and four-line Gonu the same game?

Yes. Jul-gonu played on a 4×4 line board is also called four-line Gonu. The same rules on a five-line board are Daseotjul-gonu, and on eight lines, Palpal-gonu.

Can pieces move diagonally?

No. Pieces move one step at a time along the lines, horizontally or vertically.

Am I captured if I move between two enemy pieces?

No. Only the player who moves can capture, so a piece that steps in between two enemy pieces on its own is safe.

Can a game end in a draw?

Yes, if 30 moves pass with no capture and both sides have the same number of pieces. Otherwise there is always a winner.

Can I play alone?

Yes. Play an AI on four levels — Easy, Normal, Hard and Master — or play a real person in online battle.

Is it good for children?

The rules are short and a game takes a few minutes, so it suits children and adults who are new to Gonu.
