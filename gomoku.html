How Omok works
  • Players alternate placing one stone per turn on any empty intersection of the 15×15 board.
  • Get 5 of your own stones in a row — horizontal, vertical, or diagonal — to win. No captures, no removing pieces.
  • In Freestyle, any 5-or-more in a row wins. In Standard (Renju), the first player is barred from certain "forbidden points" (double-three, double-four, six-or-more in a row) — marked with an × on the board; the second player has no such restriction. In Renju, Black's first stone goes on the center point, and broken threes and fours (with a one-point gap) count toward the forbidden shapes too.
  • If all 225 points fill up with no winner, the game is a draw.
  • Note: international tournament Renju (RIF) also restricts where the 2nd and 3rd stones go, uses an opening (swap) procedure, and makes Black lose for playing a forbidden point. PlayGonu leaves those out — forbidden points are simply marked with an × and can't be played.

Omok and Renju: where they come from

Omok is known as Gomoku in English-speaking countries and as gomokunarabe (五目並べ) in Japan, and it is played across East Asia. In Korea it is one of the most familiar games to play with a Go board and stones.

With no restrictions, the player who moves first has a large advantage. A computer proof in the 1990s showed that under Freestyle rules Black wins with perfect play. Renju is the rule set that restores balance by forbidding Black a few moves. The name Renju was given in 1899 by the Japanese journalist Kuroiwa Ruikō, and the Renju International Federation (RIF) was founded in Stockholm in 1988.

Omok is not a Gonu game, but it shares the same idea of a strategy fight on a drawn board, so we included it on PlayGonu. If you like Omok, also try Cham-gonu, where lining up three captures.

How to play Omok
  1. Start: Black moves first on the empty board. In Renju rules, Black's first move goes in the center point.
  2. Placing: On your turn, place one stone on any empty point. Stones are never moved or captured.
  3. Winning: Make five of your stones in a row, horizontally, vertically or diagonally.
  4. Freestyle: Five or more in a row (an overline) wins, and nothing is forbidden.
  5. Renju: Only Black is restricted: a double three (two open threes at once), a double four, and an overline are forbidden. Forbidden points are marked with an × and cannot be played, including threes and fours with a gap. White has no restrictions.
  6. Draw: If all 225 points fill up with no five in a row, the game is a draw.

Omok tips

  • Watch for the open three. A row of three with both ends open becomes an unstoppable open four on the next move. Block your opponent's open threes right away.
  • Aim for a four-three. A move that makes a four and an open three at once cannot be blocked on both counts. Black can use it under Renju rules too.
  • As White in Renju, use the forbidden points. If a point Black needs to play is a double-three or double-four point, Black cannot take it.
  • Fight near the center. Edges give you fewer directions to extend. In the opening, stay close to the middle of the board.

Frequently asked questions

Are Omok and Renju different games?

They are different rule sets of the same game. Renju is the official rule set that lowers Black's first-move advantage by forbidding Black a double three, a double four and an overline.

Does Black always win under Freestyle rules?

In theory, yes: with perfect play Black has been proven to win. In games between people, mistakes are common and White can win just as often.

Is this the same as tournament Renju?

Not exactly. Tournament rules add restrictions on the second and third moves and an opening (swap) procedure, and a forbidden move loses the game for Black. PlayGonu leaves these out and simply does not let Black play a forbidden point.

Is Omok a kind of Gonu?

No. Omok is a separate board game, not a member of the Gonu family.
