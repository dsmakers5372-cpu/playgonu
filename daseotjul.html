How Daseotjul-gonu works
  • Each player starts with 5 pieces lined up on their own edge of a 5x5-point board.
  • Move one piece per turn, one step along a line.
  • Trap an enemy piece between two of yours in a straight line to capture it.
  • Win by reducing your opponent to one piece, or leaving them with no legal move.
  • If 30 moves in a row (both sides counted) pass with no capture, the side with more pieces left wins (equal pieces is a draw).

Daseotjul-gonu — a Korean Traditional Board Game, Play It Online

Daseotjul-gonu (다섯줄고누, "five-line Gonu") is a traditional Korean board game for two. Each player moves five pieces one step at a time on a 5×5 line board and captures by sandwiching an enemy piece between two of their own. The rules are the same as Jul-gonu, but the board and the army are one size bigger, so planning ahead matters more. On PlayGonu you can play it against an AI right away.

Gonu and Daseotjul-gonu: where it comes from

Gonu is a family of Korean folk board games. Players draw a board on the ground or on paper and use pebbles or twigs as pieces, so no special equipment is needed. That is why the games spread across the whole country and carried regional names such as goni and kkoni (Gyeonggi), kkon and kkonu (Jeolla), kkon (Gyeongsang) and kkonja (Jeju). The name is thought to come from an old word meaning "to contend". A board carved into an artifact from a Goryeo-era celadon kiln suggests people were playing at least as far back as the Goryeo period (918–1392), and Kim Hong-do's late-Joseon genre painting "Gonu Play" shows a group playing on a board drawn in the dirt.

Games in the Jul-gonu line are named for the number of lines on the board: four lines is Jul-gonu, five lines is Daseotjul-gonu, six lines is Yeoseotjul-gonu. A version recorded in the Digital Gwangju Culture Encyclopedia also uses five pieces each, moving one step in the four directions, but there a piece is taken when two connected pieces of the mover sit in a line with one enemy piece, and the winner must capture all five enemy pieces. Capture rules and win conditions vary from region to region. PlayGonu unifies the Jul-gonu family under the sandwich-capture rule that is also the one described in English-language Wikipedia.

How to play Daseotjul-gonu
  1. Setup: Each player lines up 5 pieces on the intersections of their own front row.
  2. Moving: On your turn, slide one piece one step along a line, horizontally or vertically. No diagonal moves and no jumping.
  3. Capturing: If your move leaves an enemy piece in a straight line between two of your pieces, you remove it. One move can capture in two directions at once.
  4. The safe square: A piece that moves between two enemy pieces on its own is not captured. Only the player who moved can capture.
  5. Winning: Leave your opponent with just 1 piece, or with no piece that can move.
  6. Move limit: If 30 moves in total pass with no capture, the side with more pieces wins; equal pieces is a draw (a PlayGonu rule).

Daseotjul-gonu tips

  • The four corners cannot be captured. A corner piece has no squares on both sides to be sandwiched between.
  • Take the middle line first. On a wider board, holding the central points gives you more routes to both wings.
  • Move in pairs. Two pieces side by side protect each other and, together, work as a pair of pincers.
  • Aim at two targets in one move. A double threat can only be parried once.

Frequently asked questions

How is Daseotjul-gonu different from Jul-gonu?

The rules are identical. The board grows to five lines and each side has five pieces, which gives more room to move and more to calculate.

Can pieces move diagonally?

No. Pieces move one step at a time along the lines, horizontally or vertically.

Do all regions use the same rules?

No. A version recorded around Gwangju captures a piece with two connected pieces and requires capturing all enemy pieces to win. PlayGonu uses the sandwich-capture rule.

Can a game end in a draw?

Yes, if 30 moves pass with no capture and both sides have the same number of pieces.
