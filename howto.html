How to play Gonu

Gonu (고누) is a whole family of Korean folk board games — more than forty known variants, traditionally scratched into dirt yards rather than printed in a rulebook. PlayGonu's main Gonu game is Cham-gonu, a genuinely deep one; Pat-gonu and Jul-gonu are shorter warm-up rounds. Gomoku is a separate classic (not part of the Gonu family) added alongside them — here's how each works.

Cham-gonu 참고누 The main game
  • Each player has 12 pieces. Take turns placing one at a time on any empty point of a 24-point board (three nested squares, corners and side-midpoints connected) — this is the first half of the game.
  • Line up 3 of your own pieces in a row — a "mill" — to capture an enemy piece. A point used in a capture is marked and locked for the rest of placement.
  • Once all 24 pieces are placed, the second half begins: slide one piece per turn to an adjacent empty point, still hunting for mills.
  • Win by reducing your opponent to 2 pieces, or leaving them with no legal move. This is the same family of game as the Western "Nine Men's Morris."

Pat-gonu 밭고누 More strategy
  • Each player starts with 5 pieces lined up on their own edge of a 5×5 field.
  • A piece slides any number of empty squares in a straight line — like a rook in chess — stopping before the first piece in its path.
  • Trap an enemy piece between two of yours in a straight line to capture it.
  • Win by capturing every one of your opponent's pieces, or leaving them with no legal move.
Play Pat-gonu →
Jul-gonu 줄고누 Warm-up round
  • Each player starts with 4 pieces lined up on their own edge of a 4×4 line board.
  • Move one piece per turn, one step along a line.
  • Trap an enemy piece between two of yours in a straight line to capture it.
  • Win by reducing your opponent to one piece, or leaving them with no legal move.

Gomoku 오목 Not a Gonu variant
  • Players alternate placing one stone per turn on any empty intersection of a 15×15 board.
  • Get 5 of your own stones in a row — any direction — to win. No captures.
  • Choose Freestyle (any 5-or-more wins) or Standard (Renju), where the first player can't play certain "forbidden points" (double-three, double-four, six-or-more in a row) — the board marks them with an ×.
  • 225 moves with no winner is a draw.

Strategy tips
  • Control the center. Central points connect to more lines, so a piece there has more places to move — and more lines along which to set up a capture.
  • Watch where you walk. Moving a piece between two enemy pieces doesn't capture it — only the piece that just moved can trigger a capture. But check the reverse: don't hand your opponent a free sandwich on their turn.
  • Don't trade blind. In the capture variants, swapping pieces evenly is rarely free — it usually opens a line for whoever moves next.
  • In Cham-gonu, don't rush to form a mill during placement. A mill you can open and re-close later (by moving a piece out and straight back in) captures again and again — that's worth more than one early capture.
Playing vs AI

Every variant has a built-in computer opponent (no server, no download) with four difficulty levels. Easy mostly looks one move ahead; Master thinks several moves deep and plays a genuinely tough game. You can also choose to play first or second.
