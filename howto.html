How to play Gonu

Gonu (고누) is a whole family of Korean folk board games — more than forty known variants, traditionally scratched into dirt yards rather than printed in a rulebook. They share one shape: two players, pieces on a small board, move one at a time along drawn lines. What changes from variant to variant is the board itself, and whether pieces capture by sandwiching an enemy in a line, or simply by trapping it so it can't move. PlayGonu currently has four variants — here's how each one works.

Jul-gonu 줄고누 Easiest to learn
  • Each player starts with 4 pieces lined up on their own edge of a 4×4 line board.
  • Move one piece per turn, one step along a line.
  • Trap an enemy piece between two of yours in a straight line to capture it.
  • Win by reducing your opponent to one piece, or leaving them with no legal move.

Hobak-gonu 호박고누 Step up
  • Each player starts with 3 pieces on their own start line (the top or bottom row).
  • Move one piece per turn, one step along a line onto an empty point.
  • Once a piece leaves a start-line point, that point is gone for good — nobody can land there again, not even you.
  • No capturing here either — win by trapping every one of your opponent's pieces so none can move.
Play Hobak-gonu →
Pat-gonu 밭고누 More strategy
  • Each player starts with 5 pieces lined up on their own edge of a 5×5 field.
  • A piece slides any number of empty squares in a straight line — like a rook in chess — stopping before the first piece in its path.
  • Trap an enemy piece between two of yours in a straight line to capture it.
  • Win by capturing every one of your opponent's pieces, or leaving them with no legal move.

Strategy tips
  • Control the center. Central points connect to more lines, so a piece there has more places to move — and more lines along which to set up a capture.
  • Watch where you walk. In Jul-gonu and Pat-gonu, moving a piece between two enemy pieces doesn't capture it — only the piece that just moved can trigger a capture. But check the reverse: don't hand your opponent a free sandwich on their turn.
  • In Hobak-gonu, think in mobility, not material. It's won by trapping, not capturing — every move, ask "does this give my opponent fewer safe squares, or more?"
  • Don't trade blind. In the capture variants, swapping pieces evenly is rarely free — it usually opens a line for whoever moves next.
Playing vs AI

Every variant has a built-in computer opponent (no server, no download) with four difficulty levels. Easy mostly looks one move ahead; Master thinks several moves deep and plays a genuinely tough game. You can also choose to play first or second.
