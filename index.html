How Cham-gonu works
  • Each player has 12 pieces. Take turns placing one at a time on any empty point of the 24-point board — placement is the first half of the game. The very first placement of the game is restricted to one of the outermost square's 4 corners.
  • Line up 3 of your pieces in a row (straight, along the drawn lines) to form a "mill" — then remove one enemy piece. A point used in a capture is marked and locked for the rest of placement.
  • Once all 24 pieces are placed, the second half begins: slide one piece per turn to an adjacent empty point, still forming mills to capture.
  • Win by reducing your opponent to 2 pieces, or leaving them with no legal move.
  • Deciding who goes first (쟁두): traditionally, two players play rock-paper-scissors, and the loser gets to move first.
What is Gonu?

A family of Korean folk board games, centuries old — Cham-gonu is the deep one, same family as the Western "Nine Men's Morris." New to Gonu? Try Jul-gonu first to learn the basics.

Practice Gonu

Learn the basics in a couple of minutes

Jul-gonu 줄고누

4 pieces each on a simple line board. Step one square, sandwich to capture — the easiest way to learn how Gonu works.
Pat-gonu 밭고누

Same sandwich capture, a bigger 5×5 board, pieces slide like a rook — a bit more room to plan.
The main game
Cham-gonu 참고누

12 pieces each on a 24-point board, same family as Nine Men's Morris. Place pieces to form "mills" and capture, then slide once the board fills — genuinely deep, worth the extra few minutes to learn.
